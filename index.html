01 / About

From statistical foundations to real-world health AI.

My research interest grew from a recurring problem: health data rarely arrive in the clean, independent, and evenly sampled form assumed by standard models. Patients have different histories, measurements are missing or noisy, devices vary, and predictions must respect what was actually known at a given time.

I want to develop statistical machine learning methods that turn these complications into explicit modeling and validation questions—producing predictions that are not only accurate, but also calibrated, interpretable, and useful under uncertainty.

04 / How I work

Reliability is part of the method.

  1. 01
    Split at the patient level.

    Repeated records from one person should never leak across evaluation sets.

  2. 02
    Respect the prediction time.

    Models should only use information genuinely available at the moment of prediction.

  3. 03
    Report more than one score.

    Calibration, subgroup behavior, interval coverage, and failure cases matter alongside accuracy.

  4. 04
    State the evidence boundary.

    Retrospective performance, simulations, and prospective clinical validation are distinct claims.
