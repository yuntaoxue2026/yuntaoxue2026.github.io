About Experience Research

Research interests

Statistical machine learning for healthcare and public health.

I am interested in statistical and AI methods for longitudinal, wearable, and multimodal health data.

Working with maternal, fetal, and neonatal data has made this interest personal and concrete. I have seen how missing signals, irregular visits, small subgroups, and device differences can change a conclusion. These are not inconveniences to hide; they are part of the scientific problem. I want my research to take them seriously.

Questions that motivate me

Research questions

01

Learning from longitudinal health histories

How can we make dynamic predictions when patients are observed at different times, with different history lengths, missing measurements, and changing risk?

02

Making wearable and physiological data trustworthy

How should models represent noisy signals, combine multiple sensing modalities, recognize poor data quality, and remain robust across devices and populations?

03

Expressing uncertainty honestly

Can Bayesian reasoning, probabilistic prediction, and careful calibration turn model outputs into credible risks and intervals rather than overconfident point estimates?

04

Addressing healthcare and public health challenges

How can statistical and AI methods support earlier risk identification, reliable population-level evidence, and fairer evaluation when outcomes are rare and health data are heterogeneous?

Methodological direction

Methodological interests

I do not see statistical modeling and modern AI as competing approaches. I want to combine their strengths:

  • Statistical methods for assumptions, study design, measurement error, uncertainty, calibration, and interpretable population-level evidence.
  • Machine learning for flexible nonlinear prediction, heterogeneous effects, structured clinical variables, and robust baselines.
  • Deep learning for physiological time series, learned representations, multimodal fusion, and long patient histories.
  • Rigorous evaluation for patient-level splits, time-aware testing, subgroup analysis, rare outcomes, reproducibility, and honest limits on conclusions.

Looking ahead

Doctoral research direction

In doctoral research, I hope to study statistical machine learning for longitudinal, wearable, and multimodal biomedical data. I am especially drawn to dynamic risk prediction, time-to-event questions, uncertainty quantification, and validation under real-world heterogeneity. What keeps me motivated is the possibility of turning rigorous methods into better evidence for healthcare and public health.
